# StormSense: project knowledge for Claude

What the StormSense Claude projects have learned (Oct 2026). Since 2026-10-27 all StormSense code lives
in this private repo, DerekLoftis/StormSense-archive; the public repos StormSense-latest and
StormSense-ensemble only hold the published pages and data. Material copied from the old Tidewatch
project's shared folder (skill code, baselines, notes, station sheet, disclosures) is under `handoff/`.

## People and goal

- Owner: Derek (Jon Derek Loftis), research assistant professor at VIMS (William & Mary). Writes in English.
- StormSense is VIMS's storm tide forecast and sensor service. It grew out of Tidewatch
  (John Boon's harmonic tide + ETSS surge system on web4.vims.edu, which the old project keeps).
- Goal: archive every StormSense forecast separately from Tidewatch and develop StormSense's own 72-hour
  forecast. Derek (2026-10-27) adopted Boon's "Proposed Changes to VIMS TIDEWATCH" for StormSense: m30 line
  back on the 72-h charts, Boon's m30-locked VIMS tide instead of the P-ETSS tide, own observations (no
  web4), calibrated uncertainty, flood-stage chances, XHW peak labels, and scoring of what floods people.
  Derek confers with Dave Forrest about Tidewatch itself; StormSense is Derek's to develop as he likes.

## The run (`stormsense/`)

- `stations.csv` (generated by `tools/make_stations.py`): 77 stations, 38 from before plus 37 added
  2026-10-27 (the Tidewatch stand-in stations) and RAPV (Tappahannock, NWPS MLLW stage, P-ETSS est2345) 2026-10-28, and VIMS (Gloucester Point; stand-in alias of YRCG until the boat basin GreenStream gauge is installed) 2026-10-28. `obs` is a source chain tried in order (`coops:<id>`,
  `nwis:<site>` param 62620 NAVD88, `nwps:<LID>` stage via datum, `thredds`, `floodvision`, `greenstream`);
  `stage_datum`/`stage_to_mllw_ft` convert NWPS stages; `petss_point` (P-ETSS csv point, proxies for
  stations without one); `tidewatch_id` (the Tidewatch f36 member); `alias_of`; `suspect_obs`.
  A source whose newest reading is >2 h old is filled in after it by the next source in the chain
  (`obs._fill_stale`); USSF has `nwps:NMDV2` behind USGS (MLLW = stage - 0.112, Nov 2026 storm outage).
  GS01/GS02 have no keyless source (GreenStream API needs Derek's key). USAR = CRSM and USNR = USSF are
  the same USGS sites. Datums ft above MLLW in `data/datums/<ST>.json` (NOAA or VDatum).
- `observe.py`/`obs.py`: 40 days of every gauge's own record, QC'd, 6-minute, ft MLLW. No web4. QC: range -4..14 ft
  MLLW, rolling-median spike, step blocks (a jump > 1.5 ft + 1.5 ft/h that jumps back within 6 h, or is still open
  at the record's end; `_step_ok`, added after YRCG's 26 Nov 2026 15.4 ft NAVD plateau on web4/NWPS, which
  CO-OPS's own feed never had), stuck runs.
  `STORMSENSE_OBS_DIR` reads local `<ST>.csv` instead (testing).
- An outage is a gap, never a line (Derek, 2026-11-27): the run's `observed` column (`station.on_grid` with
  `obs_gap`: 30 minutes, or twice the gauge's own reporting step) is blank where the gauge was silent, the charts
  break their Observed and Residual lines there (`charts.pts` with `gap`), and the readers of archived runs
  (`skill.read_run`, `archive.read_run`, `crest/inputs.py`) drop the lines older runs drew (`obs.without_bridges`:
  12 or more readings in one straight line).
- `tide.py`: UTide, 27 constituents no Sa/Ssa (13 under ~6 months), stored fits `harmonics/<ST>.json`
  or fitted per run from 40 days (>=29 d). m30 lock: tide shifted so its mean over the last 30 days of
  observations equals m30 (only when >=15 days observed). m30 drawn on fore72 as a VIMS-teal line.
- `thresholds.py`: NWS categories from NWPS per gauge datum, plus `mllw`; fallback `data/thresholds_fallback.json`.
- `members_run.py` + `members.py`/`petss.py`/`etss.py`/`psurge.py`: P-ETSS, ETSS (surge only, tgftp;
  403 from the sandbox), STOFS-2D/3D, CBOFS, NWM short/medium, NWS official, NHC P-Surge, Tidewatch f36
  px30 from web4 (the one web4 read; NAVD CSV + our navd88). `station.py` lines them up with the gauge
  (median obs - member over the last 24 h, >=30 obs; NWS and Tidewatch not shifted); P-ETSS and ETSS also
  keep their surge (`petss_surge_ft`, `etss_surge_ft` in ens72) so the model puts it on our tide.
- StormSense model member (`surge.py`, `corrector.py`): gauge residual handed over (TAU 12 h) to a
  1/RMSE^2 blend of the members' surges on our tide, then the site predictor-corrector from the last week
  of archived runs. `stormsense_raw` = before the corrector (reference only).
- Primary forecast (fore72, `station.primary`): m30 tide + residual of the picked guidance per band
  (0-12/12-36/36-72, fallback P-ETSS > StormSense > ETSS > baseline, point by point), handed over from the
  gauge's current residual, eased 1 h at band edges. `data/guidance.json`: `{"mode": "adaptive"}` or
  `{"mode": "override", "override": "petss"}`. Chart names its guidance under the disclaimer.
- Cone (`uncertainty.py`): 10th/90th weighted percentiles of StormSense's own past errors (obs - fc) by
  lead hour, 30-day window, weight exp(-age/7 d), other stations x0.1; >=8 verified forecasts or it falls
  back to P-ETSS's spread. Flood chance = weighted share of whole past error trajectories added to the
  forecast whose maximum reaches the level; shown as "Action 2.39 (12%)" on NAVD charts and in fore72
  JSON `chance_of_reaching_within_72h`. The old P-ETSS cone held only 33% of obs at 0-12 h.
- XHW labels (`flood.py`): forecast high waters >= Action - 0.5 ft (NAVD charts) or > HAT (MLLW
  charts), "(XHW: 4.23)" in chart units; also `xhw_peaks` in fore72 JSON.
- Products: fore72 fields `date_time_z, astro_tide_ft, surge_ft, bias_ft, residual_ft, SSforecast_ft,
  SSforecast_p10_ft, SSforecast_p90_ft, surge_p10_ft, surge_p90_ft, m30_ft`; 7day/30day `date_time_z,
  water_level_ft, astro_tide_ft, residual_ft` (our obs, our m30 tide); ens72 one column per member plus
  `primary_ft` (+p10/p90). Every CSV has a .json twin. Metadata PDFs + JSON in `data/metadata/`
  (tools/make_metadata.py; Liberation Sans). Derek said to stop tweaking the PDFs.
- Skill (`skill.py`): every archived run x station x member scored per window: bands 0-12/12-36/36-72
  (pick), 0-36, 0-72, `full` (member's own period), 6-h segments 0-6..66-72; plus high waters (peak-height
  and timing error, hit/miss/false alarm per NWS category, `flood_scores`). Truth = the gauges' own obs;
  runs on Tidewatch's basis (no `obs_basis`) are read from their NAVD CSVs + our navd88. Last 5 days
  rescored each run (`--rescore-all` or old-format cache rescoring all). Three tables per station
  (under each ens72 chart, `skill/tables/<ST>_skill_*.csv|json`) and over all stations (`skill/`),
  ordered by SS-Skill over each forecast's period. Pick: best recent skill (10 d, e-fold 3 d), must lead by
  0.01 for 2 updates; P-ETSS until 7 days verified and >=12 scores. Never picked: tidewatch, baseline,
  tide, stormsense_raw, primary; suspect stations USSC USAR USCB USNR USSB GS01 USBB stay on P-ETSS.
- Testing locally: `PYTHONPATH=. STORMSENSE_OBS_DIR=<dir> python -m stormsense.build --now
  2026-10-27T00:20 --stations SWPT,USJT --latest /tmp/l --ensemble /tmp/e --work /tmp/w [--no-skill]`.
  A pull request runs a dry build of 9 stations (plus VIMS's YRCG) on Actions and keeps the sites as an artifact.

## CREST (`crest/`, design: https://claude.ai/artifact/3nm4xQmmyPP1BMSgBUNWob)

- Chart Review and Evaluation Statistics Tool, StormSense's counterpart of Tidewatch's TWiST (tidewatch_archive
  `twist/`). One self-contained `crest_<st>.html` per station (Highcharts + Leaflet inlined, `?embed=1`, `?datum=NAVD`,
  `?units=m`, `?period=`, `?theme=light|dark`, `#tab`), `index.html` map (Esri Ocean basemap, coastline fallback
  inlined), `feeds/` CSV + GeoJSON. `?embed=1&view=forecast#run=<cycle>&show=<member>` is the compact one-forecast
  chart the ArcGIS dashboard embeds (hash arguments re-point it without a reload); `&name=1` keeps the station name.
- ArcGIS Online (VIMS org vims-wm.maps.arcgis.com, folder "StormSense CREST", secrets `ARCGIS_URL` + `ARCGIS_USERNAME`/
  `ARCGIS_PASSWORD` or `ARCGIS_API_KEY`): `crest/schema.py` is the one list of feed columns, types, labels and guidance
  colors (the pages' colors too); `crest/arcgis_push.py` overwrites seven hosted layers in place on every run, typed
  from the schema (Stations, Daily Skill, Flood Events, Leaderboard, Lead Decay, Forecasts, Changes; read-only;
  Forecasts is ~15 MB, and a file over 5 MB that one request does not get in goes up in parts; each layer's line in
  the job log says how long it took, and a publish ArcGIS has not finished in 20 minutes is reported and left to
  finish, never sent again);
  `crest/arcgis_dashboard.py` builds the web map and the ArcGIS Dashboards item "StormSense CREST Forecast Archive" and
  creates them once (edits made in ArcGIS are kept; the workflow's `dashboard` input, `CREST_DASHBOARD=rebuild`,
  writes them again). Sharing is Derek's: never change it from code. Check dashboard edits before a PR with
  `python tools/dashboard_preview.py --site <built site>` + `node tools/dashboard_preview.js` (the real Dashboards
  app against a local stand-in for the layers; nothing is published). What that taught is in arcgis_dashboard.py's
  docstring (Arcade number patterns need `#,##0`, dates print in local time unless Arcade formats them, ...).
  The items are public (Derek, 2026-11-26): a session opens the real dashboard at
  `https://www.arcgis.com/apps/dashboards/<dashboard item id>` (the org's own host is not reachable from a session).
- Reads skill_scores/flood_scores.csv.gz (newest ensemble release), run tarballs (reduced to work/runs/<cycle>.json.gz,
  cached by actions/cache), obs-YYYY-MM day tarballs (`obs_<day>/6min/<ST>.csv`; the newest hours come from the run
  tarballs' own `observed_ft`, since the obs archive is daily), data/index/obs, NWPS levels. Periods 30d, 7d, each month;
  stations start on their `added` day. Default guidance = published fore72 (`primary`). Change markers:
  `crest/changes.csv` (add a row for each model or pipeline change). Derek's decisions 2026-11-25 are in the design doc.
- Floods (`stats.events`): observed water at or above NWS Action, exceedances under 12 h apart are one flood. A flood
  the gauge's record ends in is not over. With its highest water in the record's last 3 h it is `ongoing` (feed
  `status` "In progress", "+" in the dashboard's table): the highest water so far is listed and no guidance's peak
  error is given. After that it is `past_peak` ("Past peak"), scored against its highest water so far; below Action it
  is "Ended". The daily run sees observations to about 12:18Z, so a flood on the run's morning is complete the next
  morning.
- Local test: `python -m crest.build --out /tmp/c --work /tmp/cw --scores scores.csv.gz --floods flood_scores.csv
  --picks picks_history.csv --runs ensemble_*.tar.gz --obs-dir <dir of ST.csv> --offline --stations SWPT`.

## Inundation (`inundation/`, pilot 2026-11-25; details in `inundation/README.md`)

  centerlines cut into 5 m pieces) and VGIN building footprints. Roads over 0.5 ft deep orange, ponded (0.25 ft)

- `python -m inundation.prep --fips 51710 --name norfolk --out <dir> --work <dir> [--gdb]` once per area: each piece
  and building gets `ground_ft`, `connect_ft` (lowest level at which water reaches it), `ponded_ft`, `flooded_ft`,

  state now / at the forecast peak / at the 90% level, hours flooded, GeoJSON for hosted layers, `levels.json` for
  the area's self-contained map page (`inundation/page.py`, `templates/map.html`).
- Since 2026-11-26 (Derek's asks): water also travels through the city's stormwater pipes (`drainage.py`,
  `drainage_sources.json` per FIPS; static like Norfolk's TITAN), floodwalls from the USACE National Levee Database

  year built, assessed value (`value.py`, `value_sources.json`: Norfolk parcels + FY27 assessment roll), the ZIP's

  (EGM 01-03 / 04-01; `damage.py`), homes only. No statewide first floor elevations in Virginia: floors are assumed at the

  `bridge` 4 (2026-11-28, `prep.decks`): a deck over low ground found from the road's own profile (a fall of 12 ft
  within 15 m is an abutment; Interstate 95 at Petersburg was a flooded road at 4 ft without it).
  The Mobjack Norfolk SLR services are RGB extent pictures (no depth). Needs `inundation/requirements.txt`.
- Hampton Roads (since 2026-11-26): one `prep` per locality FIPS. `drainage_sources.json` has Norfolk, Newport

- Site (2026-11-27): `inundation/areas.csv` lists the localities (Hampton Roads, the Middle Peninsula, the Northern
  Neck and the Eastern Shore so far; add a row to add one). `python -m inundation.site --prepared <dir of <name>/> --latest <Latest site> --out site/inundation`
  writes each locality's page under `<name>/` and `index.html`, the map of all of them (`templates/statewide.html`:
  Leaflet, Esri satellite hybrid or street basemap, opens on the whole coastal zone with every gauge (Derek,
  2026-11-27; a click on a gauge puts its forecast in the plot, Sewells Point's until then), gauges colored by NWS
  flood category with a ring for the highest category forecast, water reprojected from the localities' UTM
  depth tiles, totals from `areas.json` `curves`, each flagged road piece and building from zoom 15 in, read from the
  locality's own `static.json`). `inundation.yml` runs it after every StormSense run and deploys to
  Pages at /StormSense-archive/inundation/ (release `inundation-pages`); Pages is one site per repo, so it and
  `crest.yml` each unpack the other's zip before deploying. Localities are prepared on Actions, once each (manual run,
  `prepare` = names, `missing` or `all`; release `inundation-prepared`), because a session cannot upload release assets.
  A locality that cannot be prepared does not keep the others off Pages (deploy's `!cancelled()`; the first publishing
  run, 27 Nov, built 17 localities and skipped the deploy). Newport News failed that day: the city's
  `Operational/StormwaterManhole` service had no layers, and pipes without their manholes fall apart into pieces
  whose ends count as openings. Derek (27 Nov): build it anyway. A layer with `"saved"` in `drainage_sources.json`
  that cannot be read comes from its copy under `inundation/saved_layers/` (the manholes as read 26 Nov; the log
  warns and `<name>_prep.json` lists it under `drainage.saved_layers`); a layer without a copy still stops prep.
  A manual run of `inundation.yml` from a branch prepares and builds but does not deploy (Pages takes main only).
- Tidal water (2026-11-27): the largest body at -2 ft in the frame plus every body of 5 ha or more that runs off the
  frame's edge (`depth.tidal_seeds`; before that the Piankatank side of Gloucester and the York side of James City
  were land), except one leaving inside a watershed without a gauge (`prep.sea_cells`). `spread.sparse` keeps the
  union-find's untouched pages out of memory (King and Queen's frame is 2.96 billion cells). VGIN has no footprints
  for King and Queen: FEMA USA Structures outlines stand in (`vgin.buildings`). Since 2026-11-27 the FEMA outlines
  that come within 3 m of no VGIN footprint are added everywhere (VGIN has 4,056 footprints in Northumberland where
  FEMA maps 14,101 structures, and lacks about 6,000 in King William and Gloucester and 5,000 in Mathews; table in
  the README; `outline` column, `buildings_fema` in prep.json).
- Water under a deck or a pier (`bridge` 1, `over_water`) is ground below 1 ft NAVD88 or below the gauge's mean
  higher high water where that is lower (`prep.over_water_ft`; 0.52 ft at Tangier, whose lanes lie at 0.7 to 1.5 ft
  and were all bridges under the 1 ft rule).
- Gauges by water (2026-11-27): a `tether_overrides.csv` row can name several gauges joined by `|`, and its
  watersheds take the nearest of those (Eastern Shore sea side CHIV|WACH|CCHV and bay side KIPT|ONAV|SAXV|PRTV|CRSM,
  the lower Potomac, the Rappahannock, the Mattaponi, Pamunkey, Dragon and Piankatank; table in the README). Before
  that Wachapreague was the nearest gauge to much of the bay shore, and it read 8.44 ft NAVD88 on 26 Nov against
  4.93 at Kiptopeke.
- Counties that are mostly water: `prep.land_bounds` cuts the frame to the roads and buildings plus 1.5 km,
  `spread.open_sea` gives each body of open water one union-find node (same answer), and a locality still too large
  is several rows of areas.csv with a `box` (west south east north; `prep --box`): Accomack (4.30 billion cells, over
  the 32-bit node limit) is `accomack_north`, `accomack_south` and `tangier`.
- Counties that are mostly upland (2026-11-28, Derek: "complete the remaining localities in coastal Va"): a row of
  areas.csv with `low` = 1 (`prep --low`: Henrico, Chesterfield, Hanover, Prince George) keeps only the roads and
  buildings within 1.5 km of ground at or below 10 ft that the tide can be part of (`depth.low_grid` reads one tile
  row every 64 m, `depth.tidal_low` leaves out shut-in pits such as quarry floors below sea level, `prep.near_low`
  cuts the frame). Its tables are not the whole locality. The Town of West Point is inside King William's map.
  `vgin.locality` joins a locality's rows (Prince George's first row is a 2 ha sliver). `depth.read_dem` tries a
  strip again when a read from NOAA's bucket is cut short (King William, 28 Nov). Above the head of tide (Richmond,
  Fredericksburg, Little Falls) river flooding from rain is not in these maps.
- Upriver the elevation data has a river as the water the lidar met, not its bed (the James at Richmond 0 to 2 ft,
  the Appomattox at Petersburg -1.5 ft), and the only ground at -2 ft can be a quarry (Richmond's goes to -290 ft
  and was taken for the sea in the first map). A body at -2 ft is tidal water only inside water that runs off the
  frame's edge at or below 3 ft (`prep.tidal_cells`); a frame without any gets its river lowered to -2 ft
  (`prep.tidal_surface`, `water_surface_ft` in prep.json). Read the log's "tidal water ... from" line for a new area.
- A gauge without a forecast in a run (BRDH on 27 Nov 2026) follows the nearest gauge that has one
  (`status.read_levels`; `stations_following` in the summary, `follows` in levels.json, named on the pages).
- Not wired yet: the push to the AGOL folder "StormSense CREST". Each area's prepared tables and file geodatabase are
  also in the project's shared folder under `inundation/<area>/`.

## Datums and station facts

- NOAA stations on NOAA datums (`styler/noaa_datums/<id>.json`); USGS stations plus GS01/GS02 on VDatum
  (`styler/stations_usgs/_datums/`). The 2024 Virginia VDatum releases put NAVD88 ~1 ft too high; corrected
  with an offset to MDVAchb12. Tidewatch's own MLLW-NAVD offsets differ slightly from NOAA's (DAHL 0.12,
  WMPT 0.05, BISH/YRCG 0.04, WACH 0.03 ft). NAVD charts move MLLW gauges with Tidewatch's offset.
- Retired 2026-10-15 (Derek): NN01-NN10, VB01-VB10, USNW, USPF, USSV. `DROP` in publish.py/ensemble.py.
  Never reintroduce them.
- Aliasing: Tidewatch publishes identical obs under several ids (DAHL/USNC/USSC, WADC/USAR/USBK,
  USFM/USCB, USSF/USNR, USHR/USSB, GS01/GS02). DAHL's are New Market Creek's; USSB, USCB, USNR aren't
  their sites; GS01/GS02 is GS02 (Pamunkey). USBB repeats values in ~18-min steps. USLI's f36 px30 is
  empty. Exclude Tidewatch's DAHL/WADC from skill comparisons. Details: `handoff/skill/tidewatch-asa/output/aliasing/`.
- NWPS observation datum shifts vs Tidewatch: WMPT +0.044, YRCG +0.045, BISH -0.036, WACH -0.035 ft.
- Model datum gotchas: STOFS SHEF MLLW ft; NWM SHEF NAVD88 ft and 0.6-0.7 ft low everywhere; CBOFS zeta m
  MSL (nearest node <=3 km); STOFS-2D at USGS LIDs FTMV2, JSFV2, NMDV2 sits 0.7-1.3 ft low.

## Skill scoring (reuse; code in `handoff/skill/tidewatch-asa/`)

- Derek's skill formula for every table, plot and deck: S = 0.25[R² + e^(-|Bias|/σ) + e^(-MAE/σ) +
  e^(-RMSE/σ)], σ = population SD of obs in the window; 0-1, 3 decimals, no-skill ~0.45, storm ~0.67,
  calm ~0.75. Site Skill uses the 30-day SD and CRITIC weights. Derek hasn't answered the proposal to
  merge MAE/RMSE and weight R² least; don't change the formula without him.
- Known results: Sept 2026 36-h RMSE NWS 0.24 vs Tidewatch 0.28 ft (NWS better at 10 of 11; nor'easter
  0.29 vs 0.52). King tide 8-12 Oct 2026: NWS 0.21, Tidewatch 0.26, STOFS-2D 0.18 (vs TW 0.22), CBOFS 0.25,
  STOFS-3D 0.31 (bias -0.25), NWM 0.73. First ensemble scores (runs 16-18 Oct 2026, ~1.5 ft surge event,
  mean over stations): 0-12 h RMSE ETSS 0.24, Tidewatch 0.25, STOFS-2D 0.27, P-ETSS 0.32 ft; 36-72 h STOFS-3D
  0.65, STOFS-2D 0.70, P-ETSS 0.86 ft (bias +0.77, everyone high); P-ETSS 10-90% cone held 33% of obs at 0-12 h.
- Event look-backs use Derek's deck format (`handoff/notes/flood-event-validation-format.md`): pptx with
  autoplay mp4 per forecast, station stats table first, ROYGBIV forecast colors, halo-labelled composite.
